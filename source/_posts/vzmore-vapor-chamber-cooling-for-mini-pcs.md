### Quick Report

VZMore has introduced a vapor chamber thermal architecture for mini PCs, signaling a new push toward stronger sustained performance in compact desktop hardware. The design is meant to move heat away from the chipset more efficiently than standard heat pipes and plate systems.
<!-- more -->

The main appeal is not just lower temperatures but more stable operation during heavier workloads, which matters for compact desktops carrying increasingly capable CPUs and GPUs. The architecture suggests that chassis size is no longer the limiting factor for thermal headroom when a more advanced heat spreader is used.

The implication is straightforward: mini PCs are becoming more serious performance platforms, and cooling is one of the key areas where manufacturers can keep compact systems competitive without resorting to larger enclosures. It is a design choice that could matter more as AI and workstation-class workloads become more mainstream in small form factors.
