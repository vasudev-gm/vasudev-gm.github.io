### Quick Report

Intel is reallocating some 18A manufacturing capacity away from consumer silicon and toward Xeon server production to keep pace with demand for its data-center CPUs. During its Q3 2026 earnings call, the company said the move is intended to address the sustained pressure on server demand and relieve bottlenecks in its roadmap.
<!-- more -->

The shift is a sign that Intel is still wrestling with shortages in both wafer supply and substrate capacity, even as it tries to balance mainstream desktop and laptop work with the needs of AI and enterprise infrastructure. The company said the Oregon conversion will support Intel 3-based Xeon 6 Granite Rapids production through the first half of 2027, while CEO Lip-Bu Tan expects the extra capacity to come online in the second half of 2027.

The practical impact is likely to be felt most by consumer programs, with Panther Lake supply potentially constrained as Intel prioritizes the most profitable and strategically important server lines. For the broader industry, the move underscores how demand for AI-enabled compute, not just desktop performance, is reshaping the role of advanced process nodes and packaging capacity.
