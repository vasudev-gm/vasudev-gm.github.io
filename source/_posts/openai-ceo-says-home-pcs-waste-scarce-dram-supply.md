### Quick Report

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman said the DRAM inside home PCs is a poor use of a limited global memory supply, arguing that the same capacity would be more valuable in data centers that power large AI workloads. The comments came during a podcast conversation and reflect a broader shift in how AI leaders think about memory as a strategic resource, not just a PC component.
<!-- more -->

Altman was specifically criticizing the idea that local AI inference will soon run efficiently on ordinary laptops and desktops. He argued that if AI demand keeps growing, the memory needed for personal devices may be better reserved for server clusters, where the economics and performance of large-scale model deployment are far more compelling.

The broader takeaway is that DRAM is no longer just a hardware issue for gamers and enthusiasts; it is a strategic bottleneck for AI infrastructure. If memory supply stays tight, the conflict between local inference and cloud compute will likely become one of the defining trade-offs of the next generation of AI hardware.
