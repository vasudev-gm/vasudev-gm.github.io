### Quick Report

A U.S. court has refused to dismiss a major antitrust case against Samsung, SK hynix, and Micron, keeping the allegations of coordinated DRAM pricing and supply actions alive. The ruling says the plaintiffs presented enough detail to move forward with claims that the memory makers worked together in ways that inflated prices.
<!-- more -->

The case centers on a long-running argument that major memory suppliers used shared pricing signals or coordinated supply strategies to maintain elevated DRAM margins. Judge-level review at this stage is limited to whether the complaint is plausible, not whether the underlying conduct is ultimately proven.

For buyers and investors, the decision matters because it preserves the possibility of a broader accounting of how DRAM pricing behaved across the last decade. The industry will be watching whether the case develops into a bigger challenge to the way volatile memory markets are controlled and reported.
